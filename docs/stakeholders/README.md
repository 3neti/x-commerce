# Stakeholders

## Foundational Question

```text
What is in this for them?
```

The stakeholder layer of x-commerce answers a practical ecosystem question:

```text
Why does each participant rationally choose to join and remain in the ecosystem?
```

A sustainable ecosystem cannot depend on one participant winning while the others merely tolerate it. Each participant should be able to explain:

- what value they create;
- what value they receive;
- what costs and risks they accept;
- what responsibilities they own;
- how success is measured;
- why continued participation remains rational.

The purpose is not to guarantee that everybody earns revenue. Different stakeholders may receive different forms of value: operational value, institutional value, commercial value, knowledge value, infrastructure value, capability value, relationship value, confidence value, or public value.

## Purpose

x-commerce does not treat stakeholders as entries in an organization chart.

Each stakeholder document is a stakeholder commercial thesis. It examines:

- the problem faced by the stakeholder;
- why participation may be rational;
- what the stakeholder contributes;
- what the stakeholder receives;
- its competitive or strategic position;
- its revenue or value drivers;
- leading success indicators;
- costs;
- risks;
- responsibilities;
- dependencies;
- possible five-year outcomes;
- the cost of doing nothing;
- evidence required for validation;
- contractual or regulatory work.

These documents explain why participation could become commercially, operationally, institutionally, strategically, or publicly rational. They are not contracts, legal opinions, forecasts, or guarantees.

## Canonical Structure

Stakeholder business cases use the following structure unless a future stakeholder genuinely requires a documented exception:

```text
Stakeholder
    ↓
What's In It For Them?
    ↓
Problem Today
    ↓
Why They Would Participate
    ↓
Contribution
    ↓
Receives
    ↓
Competitive Position
    ↓
Revenue Or Value Drivers
    ↓
Success Indicators
    ↓
Costs
    ↓
Risks
    ↓
Responsibilities
    ↓
Dependencies
    ↓
Five-Year Outcome
    ↓
If They Do Nothing
    ↓
Open Decisions
    ↓
Validation Evidence
    ↓
Contractual Or Regulatory Work
```

Several sections are intentionally distinct:

- Revenue Or Value Drivers asks why value could be created.
- Success Indicators asks how we know it is beginning to happen.
- Five-Year Outcome asks what a sustained result may look like.
- If They Do Nothing preserves an honest comparison rather than forcing participation.
- Validation Evidence prevents assumptions from being treated as fact.

## Ecosystem Roles Versus Organizations

The current documents use named organizations and stakeholder types because the RBAP Digital Banking Program is the first ecosystem being documented. The more reusable architecture may eventually be based on roles.

Possible emerging ecosystem roles include:

- Outcome Sponsor;
- Customer-Facing Institution;
- Institutional Convenor;
- Commercial Operator;
- Commercial Integrator;
- Knowledge and IP Steward;
- Infrastructure Partner;
- Managed Operations Partner;
- Capability Provider;
- Relationship Steward;
- Investor or Capital Steward;
- Regulator or Public-Interest Reviewer.

These roles remain observational. They are not software concepts, final commercial classifications, legal categories, or contractual roles.

Future ecosystems may contain different organizations performing the same roles. The role vocabulary should stabilize through repeated documentation before it becomes formal package grammar.

## Value Types

Revenue is not the only value created within the ecosystem.

The stakeholder documents have revealed several possible value types:

- Customer Value;
- Recipient Value;
- Institutional Value;
- Commercial Value;
- Knowledge Value;
- Infrastructure Value;
- Capability Value;
- Relationship Value;
- Confidence Value;
- Public Value.

These are emerging commercial concepts, not a final taxonomy.

They help answer:

```text
What kind of value does this stakeholder contribute or receive?
```

Do not assume that every stakeholder receives a direct monetary allocation. Some stakeholders receive efficiency, confidence, evidence, inclusion, resilience, capability, or public trust.

## Commercial Relationships

The stakeholder layer connects to the broader x-commerce commercial architecture:

```text
Stakeholder
    ↓
Ecosystem Role
    ↓
Value Created
    ↓
Outcome
    ↓
Commercial Event
    ↓
Commercial Right, if approved
    ↓
Commercial Attribution
    ↓
Commercial Waterfall, where applicable
    ↓
Stakeholder Financials
```

Clarifications:

- not every stakeholder creates a Billable Event;
- not every Commercial Event creates a Commercial Right;
- not every stakeholder participates in a Commercial Waterfall;
- not every form of value is revenue;
- investors ordinarily participate through ownership or financing rights, not transaction allocations;
- regulators and the public receive Public Value rather than ordinary commercial compensation.

## Recurring Principles

The stakeholder documents repeatedly reinforce these principles:

1. The customer purchases an outcome, not a software feature.
2. The instruction is the transaction.
3. The transaction is the product.
4. The product is the business.
5. Compensation should be earned by commercial events, not by discretion.
6. Commercial participation should be deterministic rather than discretionary.
7. The same facts should produce the same approved allocation.
8. The DevOps provider operates; the rural bank owns.
9. Provider costs must remain visible.
10. Pass-through funds, deposits, settlement balances, and gross transaction value are not automatically revenue.
11. ODTI and 3neti remain economically distinct.
12. x-commerce defines commercial architecture; x-change executes approved value-bearing instructions; x-legal determines legal characterization.
13. Regulators should not be expected to repair unclear architecture after implementation.
14. Documentation must remain faithful to actual operations.
15. Participation should be validated by evidence, not enthusiasm.

## Governance Notes

These documents are drafts.

They do not prove acceptance, endorsement, compensation, approval, licensing, legality, regulatory treatment, investment suitability, or public-sector participation.

Named individuals are examples until formally approved.

Prices are working assumptions unless supported by approved quotes or contracts.

Projections are not promises.

Stakeholder roles may change after legal, commercial, operational, accounting, tax, or governance review.

Where legal characterization is required, hand off to x-legal.

Where execution behavior is required, hand off to x-change.

Where deployable application behavior is required, hand off to the relevant application.
