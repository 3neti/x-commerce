# Business Development Partner

## Stakeholder

A business development partner is a person or institution that helps originate, develop, maintain, or expand commercial relationships for the RBAP Digital Banking Program and future ecosystem programs.

Examples may include Mike, Dens, Obbie, future institutional partners, future regional business development partners, and other relationship-oriented participants. These names are examples of stakeholder type only. This document is not evidence that any named individual or institution has accepted a role, approved compensation, received ownership rights, or entered into a contractual arrangement.

The business development partner is not the software owner, IP owner, commercial operator, bank, settlement institution, DevOps provider, legal authority, or automatic owner of customer relationships. Its role is to help build trusted commercial relationships that can become repeatable ecosystem participation.

## What's In It For Them?

The program is intended to give business development partners a transparent way to participate in long-term ecosystem growth.

This document is not about ad hoc commissions. It is about building a repeatable commercial participation model where partners are rewarded for creating durable commercial relationships, qualified opportunities, customer success, ecosystem expansion, and approved commercial events.

The guiding principle is:

```text
Compensation should be earned by commercial events, not by discretion.
```

A partner should not need to depend on goodwill, verbal promises, manual calculations, settlement-account ownership, or accounting discretion. If a commercial model is approved, participation should be based on clear rules: qualifying event, allocation basis, participating stakeholder, amount, duration, exclusions, and approval authority.

No partner compensation, allocation, attribution, ownership interest, role, duration, exclusivity, or future x-change account is guaranteed.

## Problem Today

Ecosystem growth often depends on relationships, introductions, trust, local knowledge, and long-cycle follow-through. Those contributions can be valuable but difficult to govern if the commercial model is unclear.

Without a disciplined participation model, the ecosystem risks:

- verbal promises replacing written commercial rules;
- disputed attribution among partners;
- compensation depending on discretion rather than defined events;
- partners expecting participation beyond approved economics;
- unclear customer ownership;
- overlapping claims on the same institution or program;
- one-time sales behavior replacing long-term relationship stewardship;
- manual monthly invoicing for transaction-linked participation;
- confusion between pass-through funds, platform revenue, and partner allocations.

The program is designed to make business development participation traceable, rule-based, and aligned with durable ecosystem value.

## Why They Would Participate

A business development partner participates in the business case to evaluate whether ecosystem-building work can become a long-term commercial role rather than a one-time sales event.

Potential reasons include:

- recurring participation in approved commercial events;
- transparent compensation rules;
- long-term customer relationships;
- incentive alignment with banks, ODTI, 3neti, providers, and other participants;
- repeatable income opportunities where approved;
- expanding customer or institutional portfolio;
- commercial reputation as a trusted advisor;
- participation in ecosystem growth beyond a single transaction;
- ability to help customers move from introduction to activation, usage, expansion, and retention.

These are commercial hypotheses. They must be validated through approved attribution rules, contracts, commercial events, customer adoption, operating evidence, and financial discipline.

## Contribution

The program may ask a business development partner to contribute:

- opportunity origination;
- relationship development;
- customer education;
- institutional introductions;
- regional or sector knowledge;
- proposal support;
- stakeholder coordination;
- onboarding support;
- customer-success follow-through;
- feedback from banks, sponsors, merchants, institutions, or partners;
- repeat introductions into related ecosystems;
- support for turning one relationship into a broader commercial program.

The partner should not be expected to perform undefined sales, support, implementation, legal, banking, or settlement responsibilities merely because it introduced an opportunity.

## Receives

Depending on approved arrangements, the program is intended to provide a business development partner with:

- defined participation role;
- transparent attribution rules;
- approved compensation triggers;
- possible referral, commission, advisory, success-fee, subscription, transaction-based, marketplace, or ecosystem-development participation;
- visibility into qualifying commercial events;
- durable relationship-building opportunity;
- a path to expand from one introduction to recurring ecosystem participation;
- trust that allocations follow approved rules rather than discretionary judgment.

The partner receives a possible commercial participation opportunity, not a guarantee of compensation, allocation, customer ownership, exclusivity, or continuing role.

## Competitive Position

If the program succeeds, the business development partner improves its strategic position as a builder of commercial ecosystems rather than merely a salesperson.

The intended positioning benefits include:

- long-term relationship stewardship;
- customer success orientation;
- portfolio development across banks, employers, merchants, institutions, and regional partners;
- repeat introductions based on trust and performance;
- ability to help customers navigate commercial, operational, and stakeholder questions;
- reputation as a trusted commercial advisor;
- participation in ecosystem expansion rather than isolated deal closure.

This is strategic positioning, not a claim of exclusive territory, account ownership, guaranteed commissions, or permanent role. The partner's actual position depends on attribution clarity, relationship quality, customer outcomes, approved economics, and continued trust.

An emerging reusable ecosystem role may be Relationship Steward: a participant whose primary contribution is creating and maintaining trusted commercial relationships. This document does not formalize that role. It only records the possibility for future observation across additional stakeholder documents.

## Commercial Waterfall

The Commercial Waterfall is an emerging commercial architecture concept. It is not a software implementation.

Illustrative flow:

```text
Customer
        |
        v
Commercial Event
        |
        v
Revenue Allocation
        |
        v
Commercial Waterfall
        |
        v
Stakeholder Allocations

    Rural Bank
    ODTI
    3neti
    Business Development Partner
    Value-Added Provider
    Others, if applicable
```

The waterfall should explain how value is distributed after an approved commercial event occurs. It should identify the qualifying event, allocation base, participants, amounts, duration, exclusions, taxes, provider costs, and approval authority.

Commercial participation should be deterministic rather than discretionary. When a qualifying commercial event occurs and an approved commercial model exists, the resulting commercial allocation should be determined by transparent rules rather than manual judgment.

This principle may eventually apply beyond business development partners, including referral partners, DevOps providers, marketplace providers, value-added providers, and future ecosystem participants.

## x-change Allocation Boundary

Long term, approved commercial allocations may eventually be represented as value-bearing instructions within x-change.

For example, a successful commercial event may eventually generate internal settlement instructions allocating approved amounts to participating stakeholders.

This document does not implement that model.

The boundary is:

- x-commerce defines the commercial model;
- x-change may eventually execute approved allocations;
- x-legal must review legal, accounting, banking, tax, stored-value, wallet, custody, and regulatory characterization questions before any production allocation model is implemented.

The five-year model should show business development partner economics separately from rural banks, ODTI, 3neti, NetBank, DevOps providers, value-added providers, and other partners.

Minimum partner inputs:

- attribution basis;
- allocation amount or formula;
- allocation duration;
- participating institutions introduced;

- active institutions by year;
- transaction volume tied to approved attribution;
- subscription or recurring revenue participation, if any;
- marketplace or value-added participation, if any;
- payment timing;
- exclusions;
- conflicts or overlapping partner claims;
- travel and relationship development cost;
- proposal and communications cost;
- tax treatment;
- contingency.

Required scenarios:

- Conservative: few activated institutions, limited qualifying events, higher relationship-development cost.
- Base: moderate activation, clear attribution, recurring commercial events.
- Accelerated: repeatable relationship expansion, multiple active institutions, broader ecosystem participation.

The model should show gross opportunity, approved allocation base, partner allocation, partner costs, payment timing, and net contribution. No projection should be presented as guaranteed revenue.

## If They Do Nothing

If a business development partner does not participate, they avoid travel, relationship-development cost, reputational risk, and uncertainty over future compensation.

The tradeoff is that they may miss the opportunity to help shape a repeatable ecosystem and build a durable portfolio of commercial relationships. The ecosystem may also lose local trust, introductions, market knowledge, and relationship continuity.

The "do nothing" case may be rational if attribution is unclear, compensation remains discretionary, commercial events are undefined, customer commitments are premature, or the partner's costs exceed likely approved participation.
