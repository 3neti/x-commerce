# RBAP

## Stakeholder

RBAP refers to the Rural Bankers Association of the Philippines as the proposed institutional ecosystem through which the first rural-bank digital banking program may be introduced, discussed, validated, or coordinated.

This document is a stakeholder business-case draft. It is not evidence that RBAP has endorsed the program, accepted a role, approved communications, agreed to compensation, authorized member-bank participation, or made any regulatory representation.

RBAP should be treated as institutionally distinct from 3neti, ODTI, NetBank, participating rural banks, providers, and partners.

## What's In It For Them?

The program is intended to give RBAP a structured way to help member banks evaluate digital modernization without requiring every bank to start from scratch.

RBAP's potential value is institutional: member modernization, shared knowledge, coordinated learning, common commercial language, reduced duplication, and a clearer way to discuss digital transaction services across the rural banking sector.

If formally approved, RBAP may also have referral, sponsorship, program participation, education, or coordination economics. No such economics are assumed in this document.

No RBAP endorsement, member adoption, compensation, policy position, regulatory conclusion, or program success is guaranteed.

## Problem Today

Rural banks may face similar modernization pressures but evaluate them separately, with uneven access to technical, commercial, operational, and legal analysis.

RBAP may face institutional challenges such as:

- many members needing digital service options at different levels of readiness;
- repeated vendor conversations without a common comparison framework;
- limited shared documentation for commercial assumptions, risks, and pricing models;
- uncertainty over which offerings fit rural-bank realities;
- difficulty coordinating education without appearing to endorse unapproved arrangements;
- reputational risk if member-bank modernization programs are poorly framed;
- limited visibility into member demand for specific digital transaction outcomes.

The program is designed to create a common commercial architecture that RBAP can evaluate as a possible member-value initiative without forcing every member bank into the same adoption path.

## Why They Would Participate

RBAP participates in the business case to evaluate whether the program can help member banks modernize in a coordinated, practical, and commercially disciplined way.

Potential reasons include:

- support member modernization;
- provide a common framework for evaluating digital banking opportunities;
- reduce duplicated diligence across member banks;
- create a shared vocabulary for pricing, licensing, operations, and value-added services;
- help members understand modular adoption paths such as payroll, remittance, payouts, Pay Codes, merchant payments, collections, or notifications;
- coordinate education, briefings, or member consultations if approved;
- gather member feedback on digital readiness and priority use cases;
- support rural banking relevance in a more digital financial services environment;
- explore approved referral, sponsorship, or program participation economics if appropriate.

These are institutional and commercial hypotheses. They must be validated through RBAP governance, member interest, legal review, communications discipline, and evidence from bank adoption.

## Contribution

The program may ask RBAP to contribute, if approved:

- institutional access to member-bank discussions;
- program framing and member education;
- feedback on rural-bank priorities;
- coordination of briefings, consultations, or pilot interest;
- review of member-facing communications;
- governance review of any RBAP role;
- possible referral, sponsorship, or participation structure;
- member demand signals;
- support for creating a common commercial language across participating banks.

RBAP should not be expected to endorse, sell, guarantee, regulate, supervise, or operationally support the program unless a formally approved role says so.

## Receives

Depending on approved arrangements, the program is intended to provide RBAP with:

- a structured member modernization narrative;
- a common commercial framework for digital transaction services;
- better visibility into member-bank readiness and demand;
- reusable briefing materials;
- stakeholder-specific business cases for members;
- a way to compare pricing, operating, and support models;
- possible referral, sponsorship, or program participation economics if formally agreed;
- an evidence base for future rural-bank digital service initiatives.

RBAP receives an institutional program opportunity, not a guarantee of member adoption, revenue, endorsement value, or regulatory approval.

## Competitive Position

If the program succeeds, RBAP strengthens its institutional position as a convenor of practical modernization paths for rural banks.

The intended positioning benefits include:

- helping members evaluate digital services through a shared framework;
- supporting rural-bank relevance as customer expectations become more digital;
- reducing fragmentation in member-bank modernization discussions;
- enabling member banks to adopt modular services based on readiness and local market demand;
- creating an institutional knowledge base around pricing, operations, legal dependencies, and stakeholder incentives;
- improving RBAP's ability to coordinate future digital banking economy discussions.

This is strategic positioning, not a claim of endorsement, market leadership, regulatory approval, or member adoption. RBAP's actual institutional outcome depends on member interest, governance approval, communications discipline, program performance, and the credibility of the commercial model.

## Revenue Or Value Drivers

Possible RBAP value drivers include:

- member-value creation through modernization support;
- institutional relevance and knowledge leadership;
- reduced duplication across member-bank evaluations;
- data on member needs and digital readiness;
- education or program coordination value;
- approved referral or participation economics, if formally agreed;
- sponsorship or institutional program support, if formally agreed;
- stronger ability to support future ecosystem initiatives.

The model must separate:

- RBAP institutional value;
- RBAP-approved referral or participation revenue, if any;
- rural-bank retained revenue;
- ODTI operating revenue;
- 3neti royalties or license consideration;
- NetBank rail or settlement fees;
- provider costs;
- DevOps costs;
- partner commissions;
- pass-through customer funds and settlement balances.

No RBAP revenue should be assumed unless the participation basis, payer, trigger, amount, duration, and approval authority are defined.

## Success Indicators

Early indicators that the RBAP model is working may include:

- member banks requesting briefings or follow-up discussions;
- clear segmentation of member-bank readiness;
- member banks identifying priority first use cases;
- pilot interest from qualified banks;
- reduced confusion around pricing, licensing, support, and legal dependencies;
- member feedback improving the program design;
- governance questions being surfaced early rather than after commitments;
- repeatable stakeholder materials being used across member discussions;
- RBAP communications avoiding implied endorsement before approval;
- member banks progressing from interest to structured diligence.

These are leading indicators. They are not substitutes for member adoption, approved participation economics, or program success.

## Costs

Potential RBAP costs include:

- leadership and staff time;
- member consultation and coordination;
- governance review;
- legal and communications review;
- reputation risk management;
- meeting, briefing, or event coordination;
- documentation review;
- opportunity cost if the program distracts from other member priorities;
- administrative cost if RBAP accepts a formal participation role.

RBAP should not carry operational, support, DevOps, settlement, provider, or customer-service costs unless a future approved agreement expressly assigns them.

## Risks

Key risks for RBAP include:

- implied endorsement before formal approval;
- members misunderstanding the program as mandatory or officially approved;
- unclear role between convenor, referrer, sponsor, program participant, or non-commercial educator;
- member dissatisfaction if implementation, support, pricing, or legal dependencies are poorly communicated;
- reputational risk if the program underperforms;
- regulatory sensitivity around banking terminology or customer-facing claims;
- conflict concerns if referral or participation economics are not transparent;
- uneven member readiness causing unrealistic adoption expectations;
- communications that collapse RBAP, ODTI, 3neti, NetBank, or rural-bank roles.

RBAP should not be represented as having approved or endorsed the program until its governance process says so.

## Five-Year Outcome

The five-year model should show RBAP's institutional and financial view separately from rural banks, ODTI, 3neti, NetBank, providers, DevOps operators, and partners.

Minimum RBAP inputs:

- number of member banks briefed;
- number of member banks entering diligence;
- number of member banks onboarded by year;
- RBAP role type;
- referral, sponsorship, program, or participation economics, if approved;
- payment trigger and payer, if any;
- duration of participation economics, if any;
- coordination cost;
- governance and legal review cost;
- communications cost;
- event or briefing cost;
- administrative cost;
- reputation-risk mitigation cost;
- member satisfaction or feedback indicators;
- contingency.

Required scenarios:

- Conservative: limited member interest, no participation economics, higher coordination burden.
- Base: moderate member interest, selected pilot banks, controlled coordination cost.
- Accelerated: stronger member uptake, repeatable briefings, approved participation economics if formally agreed.

The model should show member-value outcomes separately from RBAP cash economics. No projection should be presented as guaranteed revenue or guaranteed member adoption.

## If They Do Nothing

If RBAP does nothing, it avoids immediate governance, communications, reputational, and coordination risk.

The tradeoff is that member banks may continue evaluating digital services individually, with duplicated diligence and inconsistent commercial understanding. The sector may lose an opportunity to compare modular digital transaction services through a common framework.

The "do nothing" case may be rational if member interest is weak, legal terminology is unresolved, the commercial model is immature, or RBAP's governance process is not ready to evaluate a role.

## Open Decisions

- Is RBAP a convenor, referrer, sponsor, program participant, non-commercial educator, or no formal participant?
- What governance approval is required before RBAP is named externally?
- What language may be used in member communications?
- Can RBAP name, logo, events, or communication channels be used?
- Does RBAP receive referral, sponsorship, program, or participation economics?
- If economics exist, what is the payer, trigger, basis, amount, duration, and approval authority?
- Which member-bank segment should be consulted first?
- What evidence is required before RBAP hosts or supports a briefing?
- What disclaimers are required to avoid implied endorsement or regulatory conclusions?
- How should member feedback be captured and used?
