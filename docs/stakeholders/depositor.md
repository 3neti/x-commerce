# Depositor And Customer

## What's In It For Them?

Customers may receive easier access to rural-bank-branded digital transaction outcomes such as disbursements, remittances, notifications, merchant payments, collections, and program benefits.

## Contribution

Customers contribute adoption, usage, identity information where required, transaction fees where applicable, and feedback.

## Revenue Or Value

The customer does not primarily earn revenue; they receive convenience, access, evidence, and completed outcomes.

## Costs

Possible service fees, transaction fees, identity verification friction, and data-sharing obligations.

## Risks And Responsibilities

Customers may face failed transactions, delays, privacy concerns, fraud attempts, or misunderstanding of product terms.

## Dependencies

Legal disclosures, bank support, identity validation, settlement reliability, and clear customer communication.

## Five-Year Financial Outcome

Model as avoided travel cost, time saved, improved access, and fee burden by use case.

## If They Do Nothing

Customers remain dependent on existing branch, cash, or fragmented transaction channels.

## Open Decisions

- Which fees are customer-facing?
- Which fees are sponsor-facing?
- What disclosures are required?
