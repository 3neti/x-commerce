# NetBank

## Stakeholder

NetBank is a potential regulated banking, settlement, rail-access, account, API, or transaction-services participant in the proposed RBAP Digital Banking Program.

This document is a stakeholder business-case draft. It is not evidence that NetBank has accepted a role, approved pricing, agreed to provide settlement or rail services, endorsed the program, or made any regulatory representation.

NetBank should be treated as institutionally and economically distinct from RBAP, ODTI, 3neti, participating rural banks, providers, DevOps operators, and partners.

## What's In It For Them?

The program is intended to evaluate whether NetBank can participate as a regulated banking and settlement partner for rural-bank digital transaction outcomes.

NetBank's possible value is not simply "more transactions." Its role may include settlement relationships, regulated rail usage, account relationships, API utilization, banking service fees, transaction activity, and strategic participation in a rural-bank digital banking economy.

If the model works, NetBank may support completed transaction outcomes while preserving clear boundaries around regulated responsibilities, settlement balances, pass-through funds, and actual earned revenue.

No NetBank role, fee, account relationship, settlement volume, deposit balance, regulatory approval, or transaction activity is guaranteed.

## Problem Today

Digital rural-bank programs may need regulated banking participation, settlement support, rail access, account structures, or payment operations. Without role clarity, the program risks confusing technical transaction flows with regulated banking responsibilities.

NetBank may face constraints such as:

- unclear role definition across settlement, account, rail, API, and banking services;
- risk that pass-through settlement balances are mistaken for revenue;
- unclear responsibility for transaction failures, reversals, disputes, or customer support;
- integration and compliance workload that may exceed commercial benefit;
- regulatory sensitivity around product language, customer disclosures, and account relationships;
- unclear allocation of responsibilities among NetBank, rural banks, ODTI, 3neti, and providers;
- uncertainty over transaction volume, fee basis, support burden, and operational risk.

The program is designed to identify NetBank's possible role before settlement, rail, API, or banking-service assumptions are presented externally.

## Why They Would Participate

NetBank participates in the business case to evaluate whether the program can create a commercially and regulatorily coherent banking-services opportunity.

Potential reasons include:

- support settlement or rail usage for rural-bank digital services;
- develop banking relationships with participating rural banks;
- support account structures where legally and operationally appropriate;
- generate approved banking, settlement, rail, API, or transaction service fees;
- increase API utilization or transaction activity;
- participate strategically in rural-bank digital capability development;
- support new use cases such as payroll, remittance, collections, merchant payments, or government payouts;
- gather operating evidence for repeatable banking-service participation in future ecosystems.

These are commercial and institutional hypotheses. They must be validated through NetBank review, legal characterization, compliance analysis, pricing approval, operating scope, integration effort, and actual transaction behavior.

## Contribution

The program may ask NetBank to contribute, if approved:

- regulated banking or settlement participation;
- account structures or settlement accounts;
- rail access or transaction processing support;
- API access or integration support;
- compliance and risk review;
- fee schedule input;
- operational requirements;
- transaction monitoring or reporting, if applicable;
- dispute, reversal, or exception-handling processes;
- review of customer-facing and bank-facing language where NetBank's role is referenced.

NetBank should not be expected to provide undefined banking, settlement, compliance, support, liquidity, credit, fraud, customer-service, or regulatory obligations without approved scope, contracts, controls, and economics.

## Receives

Depending on approved arrangements, the program is intended to provide NetBank with:

- defined banking or settlement participation;
- potential account relationships with participating rural banks;
- approved settlement, rail, API, transaction, or banking-service fee opportunities;
- increased transaction activity, if adoption occurs;
- better visibility into rural-bank digital service demand;
- repeatable operating evidence for future programs;
- clearer role documentation with rural banks, ODTI, 3neti, and providers.

NetBank receives a possible regulated banking-services opportunity, not a guarantee of transaction volume, deposits, balances, fees, profitability, or regulatory treatment.

## Competitive Position

If the program succeeds, NetBank improves its strategic position as a banking-services participant for rural-bank digital transaction ecosystems.

The intended positioning benefits include:

- becoming a clearer settlement or banking-services option for rural-bank digital programs;
- supporting rural banks without replacing their institutional customer relationships;
- participating in modular use cases such as payroll, remittance, merchant payments, collections, and government payouts;
- demonstrating API, settlement, or regulated service capability in a repeatable program structure;
- developing operating knowledge that may apply to future ecosystems;
- strengthening institutional relationships with rural banks and program operators.

This is strategic positioning, not a claim of market leadership, regulatory approval, deposit growth, or guaranteed transaction volume. NetBank's actual position depends on approved role scope, operating reliability, legal characterization, transaction activity, compliance controls, and stakeholder trust.

## Revenue Or Value Drivers

Possible NetBank value drivers include:

- approved banking-service fees;
- settlement or rail fees;
- account service fees;
- API usage fees, if approved;
- transaction processing fees;
- institutional relationships with participating rural banks;
- strategic participation in a rural-bank digital network;
- operating evidence for future ecosystem programs.

The model must separate:

- gross transaction value;
- pass-through customer funds;
- settlement balances;
- customer deposits or bank balances;
- NetBank earned fees;
- rural-bank retained revenue;
- ODTI platform or transaction charges;
- 3neti royalties or license consideration;
- provider costs;
- DevOps costs;
- taxes;
- reversals, refunds, disputes, and exception costs.

Settlement balances, deposits, float, and pass-through funds must not be treated as NetBank revenue. Only approved fees, spreads, charges, or other recognized income should be shown as NetBank revenue, subject to legal, regulatory, and accounting review.

## Success Indicators

Early indicators that the NetBank model is working may include:

- NetBank role definition approved for discussion;
- fee basis and service scope documented;
- participating banks understanding NetBank's role;
- settlement or rail flows mapped clearly;
- pass-through balances separated from revenue in reports;
- integration requirements understood before launch;
- transaction completion rate within expected range;
- exception, reversal, or dispute volume within planned tolerance;
- support escalation paths working in practice;
- compliance questions surfaced early;
- transaction activity increasing without role confusion;
- repeatable documentation for future bank or ecosystem programs.

These are leading indicators. They are not substitutes for approved fees, collected revenue, regulatory comfort, or operating profitability.

## Risks

Key risks for NetBank include:

- role mischaracterization;
- implied endorsement before approval;
- regulatory exposure from unclear product language;
- settlement balances or pass-through funds being misrepresented as revenue;
- customer confusion over whether NetBank, the rural bank, ODTI, or another party owns support responsibility;
- transaction failures or disputes without clear escalation paths;
- API, rail, or settlement outages affecting program reputation;
- compliance workload exceeding expected transaction economics;
- unclear responsibility for KYC, AML, fraud, reversals, sanctions screening, or customer disclosures;
- support burden exceeding fee revenue;
- NetBank role being collapsed into ODTI, 3neti, RBAP, or rural-bank responsibilities.

NetBank should not be represented as having approved or endorsed the program until its own governance process says so.

## Five-Year Outcome

The five-year model should show NetBank's economics separately from rural banks, ODTI, 3neti, RBAP, providers, DevOps operators, and partners.

Minimum NetBank inputs:

- approved role type;
- number of participating rural banks using NetBank-supported flows;
- account setup assumptions, if applicable;
- transaction volume by use case;
- settlement or rail fee basis;
- API or banking-service fee basis, if any;
- account service fees, if any;
- operating cost per bank or transaction;
- integration cost;
- compliance and risk-review cost;
- support and exception-handling cost;
- dispute, reversal, refund, or failed transaction assumptions;
- reporting and reconciliation cost;
- taxes;
- contingency.

Required scenarios:

- Conservative: limited bank adoption, low transaction volume, higher compliance or support burden.
- Base: moderate bank adoption, clear role scope, controlled operating cost.
- Accelerated: stronger transaction activity, repeatable integration, stable fee and support model.

The model should show approved fees and recognized income separately from gross transaction value, settlement balances, deposits, float, and pass-through funds. No projection should be presented as guaranteed revenue or guaranteed regulatory treatment.

## If They Do Nothing

If NetBank does not participate, it avoids immediate legal, compliance, integration, support, and operational risk.

The tradeoff is that participating rural banks and ODTI may need another settlement, rail, account, or banking-services participant. NetBank may also lose an opportunity to learn from rural-bank digital transaction flows and develop repeatable regulated service participation for future ecosystems.

The "do nothing" case may be rational if the role is unclear, transaction economics are weak, compliance risk is too high, support boundaries are unresolved, or legal characterization is not ready.
