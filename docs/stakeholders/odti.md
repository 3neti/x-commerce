# ODTI

## Stakeholder

ODTI is the proposed commercial operator of the technology licensed from 3neti for the RBAP Digital Banking Program and future ecosystem programs.

This document is a stakeholder business-case draft. It is not evidence that ODTI has accepted a final operating role, approved pricing, agreed to contracts, committed to provider arrangements, or received legal or regulatory clearance.

ODTI must be treated as economically distinct from 3neti. ODTI may operate the commercial program, but 3neti remains the intellectual-property owner unless a future approved structure says otherwise.

## What's In It For Them?

The program is intended to give ODTI a repeatable commercial operating model for packaging, licensing, deploying, supporting, and monetizing digital transaction outcomes across rural banks and future ecosystems.

ODTI's opportunity is not simply to resell software. It is to coordinate a commercial platform where successful transaction outcomes can become commercial events: licensing events, onboarding events, transaction-fee events, value-added service events, managed operations events, provider-participation events, and stakeholder-value events.

If the model works, ODTI can build recurring platform economics while rural banks retain their own customer-facing brand and 3neti continues developing and licensing the intellectual property.

No ODTI revenue, margin, adoption level, bank pipeline, provider markup, or renewal rate is guaranteed.

## Problem Today

Without a structured commercial operating model, the ecosystem risks having technology without a business system.

ODTI may face practical constraints such as:

- unclear licensing and contracting structure with rural banks;
- undefined relationship with 3neti as IP owner;
- fragmented pricing assumptions across licensing, transactions, value-added services, and managed operations;
- provider costs that could be mistaken for platform revenue;
- unclear DevOps accountability and support boundaries;
- partner or referral expectations that are not yet formalized;
- lack of stakeholder-specific financial models;
- difficulty turning one rural-bank opportunity into a repeatable program.

The program is designed to give ODTI a disciplined commercial architecture before sales activity, partner commitments, or implementation obligations outrun the model.

## Why They Would Participate

ODTI participates in the business case to evaluate whether it can become the commercial operator for a repeatable digital banking economy.

Potential reasons include:

- build recurring platform revenue through approved licensing or subscriptions;
- earn implementation, onboarding, or activation revenue where approved;
- earn transaction-linked platform charges;
- coordinate value-added services while separating provider costs from markup;
- package managed cloud operations through a direct, resale, or bundled model;
- develop rural-bank program expertise that can transfer to other ecosystems;
- coordinate provider, DevOps, channel, and business-development participation;
- create stakeholder-specific commercial materials for banks, RBAP, NetBank, 3neti, providers, and partners;
- turn the first bank or cohort into a repeatable operating playbook.

These are commercial hypotheses. They must be validated through bank adoption, provider quotes, support costs, legal review, contracting discipline, operating performance, and renewal behavior.

## Contribution

The program asks ODTI to contribute:

- commercial operation and program management;
- rural-bank contracting and account coordination;
- pricing proposal development;
- implementation and onboarding coordination;
- provider selection and commercial coordination;
- DevOps model selection and management, if ODTI resells or bundles operations;
- transaction and value-added service packaging;
- support-boundary design;
- stakeholder financial model development;
- partner and referral formalization;
- reporting and commercial evidence;
- coordination with 3neti on IP license, royalties, and package evolution;
- handoff of legal characterization questions to x-legal.

ODTI should not accept undefined support, security, DevOps, settlement, provider, or regulatory obligations without a documented scope and matching economics.

## Receives

Depending on approved arrangements, the program is intended to provide ODTI with:

- a commercial operating role for the RBAP Digital Banking Program;
- a contracting path to participating rural banks;
- licensing, subscription, activation, or maintenance revenue opportunities;
- platform or transaction charge opportunities;
- value-added service packaging and markup opportunities;
- managed operations resale or bundled-service opportunities;
- professional service or onboarding revenue opportunities;
- provider and partner coordination leverage;
- stakeholder-specific commercial evidence;
- a repeatable model that may later apply to other ecosystems.

ODTI receives a commercial operating opportunity, not a guarantee of adoption, revenue, profitability, or exclusivity.

## Competitive Position

If the program succeeds, ODTI improves its strategic position as a commercial operator for digital transaction ecosystems rather than a one-off project vendor.

The intended positioning benefits include:

- becoming the operating layer between 3neti IP and participating institutions;
- building repeatable expertise in rural-bank digital services;
- coordinating modular offerings that can begin with one use case and expand over time;
- demonstrating provider and DevOps orchestration capability;
- creating a reusable commercial model for future ecosystems beyond RBAP;
- developing stakeholder-facing financial and operating evidence;
- avoiding dependence on a single software license sale by building recurring and transaction-linked economics.

This is strategic positioning, not a claim of market leadership. ODTI's actual competitive outcome depends on adoption, service quality, pricing discipline, legal readiness, support execution, provider reliability, and the ability to maintain trust among 3neti, banks, RBAP, NetBank, providers, and partners.

## Revenue Or Value Drivers

Possible ODTI value drivers include:

- license or subscription revenue from rural banks;
- onboarding, activation, implementation, or professional service revenue;
- annual maintenance revenue, where approved;
- platform or transaction charges;
- value-added service markup after provider cost;
- managed cloud resale margin, if ODTI contracts and resells operations;
- bundled managed operations economics, if ODTI pays a wholesale provider;
- reporting, audit, reconciliation, or premium support services;
- marketplace coordination economics, if approved later;
- channel, institutional, or partner-led bank pipeline;
- reusable commercial documentation and operating playbooks.

The model must separate:

- gross transaction value;
- pass-through customer funds;
- bank-retained revenue;
- ODTI gross revenue;
- provider costs;
- DevOps provider costs;
- NetBank rail or settlement charges;
- taxes;
- refunds, credits, bad debt, and support costs;
- 3neti royalties or license consideration;
- partner commissions, referral fees, or success fees where approved;
- ODTI net platform revenue and net contribution.

The full selling price of SMS, email, KYC, rider, identity, reporting, or other third-party services must not be treated as ODTI revenue unless provider costs and other allocations are separated.

## Success Indicators

Early indicators that ODTI's model is working may include:

- rural banks progressing from inquiry to signed proposal;
- short and repeatable onboarding time;
- first-use-case selection becoming easier across banks;
- completed transactions growing after launch;
- repeat usage by customers or sponsors;
- value-added service attachment rate;
- provider costs matching assumptions;
- support tickets staying within planned load;
- incident response and escalation boundaries working in practice;
- bank satisfaction with onboarding and reporting;
- renewals or expansion from one module to additional modules;
- commercial reports that separate provider costs, pass-through amounts, and ODTI revenue cleanly.

These indicators are leading signals. They are not substitutes for revenue, margin, cash collection, or profitability analysis.

## Costs

Potential ODTI costs include:

- 3neti license consideration or royalties;
- sales and business-development costs;
- proposal and contracting costs;
- onboarding and implementation coordination;
- support operations;
- DevOps provider costs, if ODTI resells or bundles managed operations;
- value-added service provider costs;
- reporting, reconciliation, and audit support;
- partner commissions, referral fees, or success fees where approved;
- administration, finance, billing, and collections;
- tax, bad debt, refunds, and credit exposure;
- legal review and contract management;
- contingency for incidents, delays, or support overruns.

ODTI should model direct provider costs before claiming margin.

## Risks

Key risks for ODTI include:

- treating pass-through funds or provider selling prices as ODTI revenue;
- underpricing implementation, support, or managed operations;
- carrying DevOps accountability without adequate margin or control;
- unclear split between ODTI operating revenue and 3neti IP economics;
- overcommitting to rural banks before legal, support, provider, and settlement dependencies are ready;
- partner compensation disputes;
- low bank adoption or slow transaction growth;
- value-added service adoption below assumptions;
- provider outages or cost changes;
- support burden exceeding subscription, maintenance, or transaction revenue;
- legal terminology or regulatory characterization changing the product narrative;
- inability to collect fees on time from banks, sponsors, or other payers.

ODTI should not rely on gross transaction value as evidence of business performance.

## Five-Year Outcome

The five-year model should show ODTI's economics separately from 3neti, participating rural banks, NetBank, providers, and partners.

Minimum ODTI inputs:

- banks onboarded by year;
- active banks by year;
- license or subscription model;
- onboarding or activation revenue;
- annual maintenance revenue;
- transaction volume by bank and use case;
- platform or transaction charge;
- value-added service adoption rate;
- value-added service customer-facing price;
- direct provider cost;
- gross markup and margin;
- DevOps setup and monthly cost;
- managed operations resale or bundle price, if applicable;
- ODTI support cost;
- sales and administration costs;
- 3neti royalty or license consideration;
- RBAP participation, if approved;
- partner commissions, referral fees, or success fees;
- taxes;
- bad debt, refunds, and credits;
- churn and renewal rates;
- contingency.

Required scenarios:

- Conservative: slower bank adoption, lower transaction volume, lower value-added attachment, higher support cost.
- Base: moderate bank adoption, practical onboarding, selective value-added services, controlled support burden.
- Accelerated: repeatable onboarding, stronger transaction growth, higher service attachment, improved operating leverage.

The model should show gross revenue, provider costs, gross margin, operating costs, royalties, commissions, taxes, bad debt, net contribution, and cash timing. No projection should be presented as guaranteed revenue.

## If They Do Nothing

If ODTI does not develop this commercial operating model, it may avoid immediate sales, contracting, provider, support, and operational risk.

The tradeoff is that the technology may remain difficult to commercialize consistently. Rural-bank opportunities may become one-off negotiations, provider costs may remain unclear, partner expectations may drift, 3neti IP economics may remain under-defined, and the ecosystem may lack a repeatable answer to "What is in this for me?"

The "do nothing" case should be evaluated honestly. If adoption evidence, legal readiness, support capacity, or provider economics are weak, delaying commercialization may be rational.
