## Executive Summary

Payroll Starter gives the Rural Bank a structured way to earn from employer payroll relationships while building digital-service capability. The Level 1 model suggests Payroll can become incrementally attractive, but it does not justify the full rural-bank-owned platform by itself at current candidate volumes.

The key distinction is:

```text
Incremental Payroll Economics
    +
Modernization Portfolio
    +
Full-Cost Stress Test
```

## Your Role

The Rural Bank is the customer-facing institution, baseline collection party, and owner of the production environment. It collects employer commercial fees, retains approved bank economics, pays approved downstream obligations, and owns the broader modernization decision.

## Revenue Sources

The Rural Bank revenue shown in the Core Payroll model comes from:

- share of employer onboarding fees;
- share of employer monthly payroll-service fees;
- retained recipient-disbursement economics.

Optional SMS margin is separate. Payroll funding remains pass-through and is not revenue.

## What The Numbers Mean

Payroll itself starts to show useful incremental economics when employer relationships grow. Employer monthly service fees matter more than recipient transaction retention.

The full-cost stress test remains negative because platform subscription, DevOps, cloud, monitoring, backups, security, and common infrastructure are modernization costs. They should be evaluated across a portfolio of offerings, not forced onto Payroll alone.

## What Is Still Missing

- `RB-002`: true Rural Bank internal payroll-support cost.
- `NET-001`: NetBank or infrastructure fee.
- `TAX-001`: tax and withholding treatment.
- `COL-001`: collection timing and cash availability.
- Pilot evidence for employer adoption, support burden, exception handling, and recurring usage.

`RB-002` is the most important bank-specific blocker. Until it is evidenced or provisionally authorized, the visible bank line is not true net incremental profitability.

## Risks

- Employer acquisition may be slower than the Base or Accelerated cases.
- Internal bank support could be higher than expected.
- Payroll alone may be asked to carry modernization costs that belong to multiple offerings.
- NetBank fees, taxes, or compliance costs may reduce bank economics.
- Conservative conditions remain weak.

## Opportunities

- Build recurring employer relationships.
- Use Payroll as the first repeatable digital-service offering.
- Add future services on the same platform, including remittance, collections, government payouts, reimbursements, and incentives.
- Convert payroll relationships into broader depositor, employer, and community relationships without treating deposits as revenue.

## Key Decisions

- How much employer payroll demand can the bank realistically develop?
- Which platform costs are modernization investments rather than Payroll-only costs?
- What internal support model is required for employer onboarding and recurring payroll operations?
- Which future offerings should share the same infrastructure?

## Questions This Stakeholder Should Ask

1. How many employer payroll customers can we realistically acquire?
2. Can we justify the modernization investment across multiple offerings?
3. How much employer support will we actually need?
4. Which costs are caused by Payroll, and which costs belong to the bank's broader digital platform?
5. What evidence would allow `RB-002` to become usable?
6. What other offerings should be modeled before judging the full platform payback?
