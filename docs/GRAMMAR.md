# Commercial Grammar

This document defines how x-commerce uses commercial terms. It is not a legal, tax, or accounting opinion.

It is also not a software interface catalog. The terms below are commercial architecture concepts first. Some may later become reusable package primitives, but only after documentation, stakeholder models, and operating evidence show that they deserve first-class status.

## Governing Rule

An idea is not an assumption. An assumption is not a price. A price is not a contract. A projection is not a promise.

Availability is not authority; authority is not applicability; applicability is not implementation.

Every successful transaction creates an opportunity for multiple participants to create and share value.

## What x-commerce Is Learning To Name

x-change names how value-bearing instructions are represented, authorized, executed, completed, and evidenced.

x-commerce names how completed outcomes become commercially sustainable.

The central shift is this:

```text
Execution succeeds
-> An outcome is delivered
-> A commercial event may be recognized
-> Value may be allocated among participants
-> The ecosystem becomes more sustainable
```

This grammar should help future documents answer:

- What outcome was delivered?
- Who values that outcome?
- Which participant contributed to it?
- Which cost was incurred?
- Which fee, markup, royalty, commission, or share is justified?
- Which value is pass-through and which value is revenue?
- Which assumptions remain unresolved?

## Core Flow

Current working grammar:

```text
Ecosystem
-> Program
-> Stakeholder
-> Outcome
-> Commercial Offering
-> Commercial Event
-> Value Flow
-> Price / Rate Card
-> License or Subscription
-> Usage / Billable Event
-> Provider Cost
-> Markup / Margin
-> Revenue Participant
-> Revenue Share / Royalty / Commission
-> Stakeholder Financials
-> Evidence and Review
```

This is a working grammar, not a database schema or PHP interface list.

## Digital Banking Economy

The RBAP Digital Banking Program is the first ecosystem documented by x-commerce, but the grammar is larger than RBAP.

x-commerce is gradually describing a digital banking economy: a repeatable commercial structure where successful transaction outcomes can create value for customers, participating institutions, operators, IP owners, settlement participants, providers, DevOps operators, partners, and future ecosystem participants.

Future ecosystems should feel like natural extensions of the same grammar, not separate inventions. Examples include cooperatives, thrift banks, microfinance institutions, government disbursement programs, enterprise payroll networks, remittance networks, merchant ecosystems, healthcare settlement networks, education and scholarship programs, and regional or international banking alliances.

The following are candidates, not conclusions:

- Outcome;
- Commercial Event;

- Revenue Participant;

- Value Flow;

Do not introduce PHP interfaces, models, migrations, pricing engines, checkout systems, catalogs, or billing abstractions merely because these terms appear here. Let repeated documentation use prove which concepts deserve implementation.

## Terms

Ecosystem: a network of institutions, customers, operators, providers, partners, and regulators around a repeatable commercial opportunity. RBAP is the first ecosystem documented by x-commerce, not the package boundary.

Program: a structured commercial initiative within an ecosystem, such as the RBAP Digital Banking Program. A program has stakeholders, outcomes, assumptions, pricing models, operating models, risks, and decision records.

Commercial architecture: the structure that explains how an outcome is packaged, priced, licensed, sold, metered, commissioned, shared, and sustained.

Commercial chain: the explicit relationship among participants that explains IP ownership, commercial operation, licensing, customer-facing delivery, provider participation, and revenue allocation.

Stakeholder: a person, institution, provider, operator, partner, investor, regulator, or public-interest participant whose incentives, contributions, costs, risks, and benefits must be understood.

Revenue participant: a stakeholder that may receive approved revenue, royalty, commission, referral fee, markup, service fee, or other commercial consideration from a defined commercial model.

Outcome: the completed customer result being purchased, such as a completed payroll disbursement, delivered remittance, verified subsidy, merchant settlement, KYC-qualified payout, beneficiary notification, post-transaction call to action, or feedback-bearing transaction.

Commercial event: a successfully delivered outcome that may create commercial value for one or more participants. A commercial event is not the same as execution. Execution belongs to x-change; commercial value allocation belongs to x-commerce.

Commercial offering: a sellable package of outcomes, rights, services, and responsibilities. It may include software access, implementation, transaction services, managed operations, support, and value-added services.

Marketplace item: a future sellable or selectable item in a marketplace or service menu. It may be a value-added service, transaction rider, provider service, reporting service, support tier, or other approved commercial item. Deferred in this phase.

Catalog item: a future sellable item in a product catalog. Deferred in this phase.

Value flow: the commercial path by which value is created, priced, paid, retained, passed through, marked up, shared, or evidenced. A value flow must not collapse customer funds, settlement balances, pass-through costs, fees, and revenue.

Money flow: the movement of funds among parties. A money flow may include pass-through funds, provider costs, service fees, royalties, commissions, settlement fees, rail fees, taxes, or net revenue. It must identify which amount type is being shown.

License: permission to use intellectual property or software under defined terms.

Sublicense: downstream permission granted under an upstream license, if permitted by contract.

Royalty: consideration paid to an IP owner or rights holder according to an approved basis.

Maintenance: recurring support, updates, or upkeep tied to a licensed technology.

Subscription: recurring access to a platform, service, or commercial offering for a defined period.

Activation: commercial or technical enablement of a customer, bank, program, or service.

Onboarding: the process of preparing an institution, partner, or customer to use an offering.

Implementation: professional work required to deploy, configure, integrate, train, migrate, or launch.

Professional service: scoped human service work that is separately priced or bundled.

Managed service: recurring operational responsibility performed by a provider.

Usage: measurable consumption of a service or capability.

Metering: recording usage in a form that can support reporting, billing, limits, or analysis.

Billable event: a usage event that an approved commercial model treats as chargeable. A billable event may be related to a commercial event, but the two are not identical: a commercial event describes value creation, while a billable event describes chargeability.

Base transaction fee: the platform or service charge for a completed disbursement or qualifying transaction.

Provider cost: direct cost charged by a third-party service provider, rail, infrastructure provider, or operator.

Markup: the difference between provider cost and customer-facing price before other allocations.

Margin: retained economic value after applicable direct costs, expressed as amount or percentage.

Commission: compensation for selling, originating, or supporting a commercial transaction.

Referral fee: compensation for introducing a customer, partner, or program opportunity.

Success fee: compensation contingent on an approved result, such as contract signing, activation, or revenue collection.

Revenue share: contractual allocation of defined revenue among participants.

Marketplace: a future commercial environment where offerings or value-added services may be listed, selected, priced, and settled.

Value-added service: an optional or bundled service attached to a transaction, such as SMS, email, KYC, OTP, digital signature, selfie, face match, location verification, feedback, surveys, document collection, fraud analysis, reporting, archival evidence, reconciliation, or premium support.

Rider: post-transaction content, URL, call to action, survey, campaign, or information attached to a transaction. The billable unit for a rider may be per transaction, per campaign, per placement, per response, or another approved model.

Entitlement: future right to access a feature, service, license tier, limit, or support level.

Edition: future package variant or tier.

Bundle: grouped offering of products, services, or entitlements.

Quote: proposed commercial terms that are not binding unless accepted under an approved process.

Order: accepted purchase request or commercial instruction. Deferred in this phase.

Invoice: request for payment under approved commercial terms. Deferred in this phase.

Settlement fee: charge for regulated settlement activity or settlement support.

Rail fee: charge imposed by a payment, banking, transfer, or settlement rail.

Gross transaction value: total value moved or processed. It is not revenue unless a fee or margin is earned.

Revenue: earned company income under an approved accounting treatment.

Float: funds temporarily held or available before settlement. Do not assume ownership, revenue, or legal permissibility.

Deposit: a regulated banking concept. Do not use loosely; hand off to x-legal.

Treasury allocation: allocation of funds for treasury, settlement, liquidity, or operating purposes, subject to legal and accounting review.

Assumption: explicit working input used for analysis before approval.

Scenario: structured combination of assumptions used to compare possible outcomes.

Projection: calculated view based on assumptions. It is not a promise.

Forecast: management estimate that may require formal approval and evidence. Avoid using this label casually.

Business case: stakeholder-specific explanation of contributions, benefits, costs, risks, dependencies, and projected outcomes.

Stakeholder financials: financial view prepared for one participant, showing their own revenue, costs, risks, and dependencies.

## Commercial Event Grammar

A commercial event should eventually be able to answer:

- What outcome was completed?
- Which x-change execution evidence supports completion?
- Who purchased, sponsored, or consumed the outcome?
- Which stakeholder created value?
- Which stakeholder received value?
- Which provider cost, rail fee, or operating cost was incurred?
- Which fee or markup was charged?
- Which participant may receive revenue, royalty, commission, referral fee, or share?
- Which amount is pass-through?
- Which assumption, price, contract, or decision authorizes the model?

This does not mean x-commerce should implement commercial events now. It means future documents should become precise enough that implementation, if approved later, is grounded in a stable commercial grammar.

## Package Boundary Rules

- x-change executes transactions.
- x-commerce explains how completed transaction outcomes become commercially sustainable.
- x-legal reviews legal characterization and regulatory meaning.
- Applications assemble execution and approved commercial primitives into deployable products.

Do not move execution semantics into x-commerce.

Do not move commercial pricing, revenue sharing, or stakeholder financial logic into x-change without a future extraction decision.

Do not treat this grammar as a mandate to build software abstractions.

## Usage Rules

- Do not call pass-through funds revenue.
- Do not call provider selling price platform revenue unless provider cost and markup are separated.
- Do not call assumptions approved prices.
- Do not call projections guaranteed outcomes.
- Do not call informal partner models contractual rights.
- Do not use banking terms as product shorthand without x-legal review.
- Do not treat a successful execution as automatically billable.
- Do not treat a billable event as automatically profitable.
- Do not treat a commercial event as a legal conclusion.
- Do not collapse 3neti and ODTI into a single economic entity.
